TD3 on TrackMania: what the logs show

31 Oct 2026 · Based on the experiment manifests and metrics committed in the repository (recorded results, not re-run).

These are notes on the TrackMania TD3 project: what the algorithm needs, what broke when a behaviour-cloned policy met off-policy RL, and why the current result is reported as a plateau. Every number below comes from the experiment manifests and metrics CSV committed in the repository. Nothing was re-run for this note.

What TD3 adds to DDPG

The project started with DDPG and moved to TD3 after unstable behaviour. TD3 makes three changes, and all three are in td3/agent.py:

  1. Twin critics, clipped double-Q. The target is r + γ·min(Q1′, Q2′), which counters the over-estimation a single critic builds up.
  2. Target policy smoothing. Clipped Gaussian noise (clip 0.5) is added to the target action, so the critic cannot exploit narrow peaks in Q.
  3. Delayed policy updates. The actor and the target networks update once every two critic updates (policy_delay = 2); targets are Polyak-averaged with tau = 0.005.

TMRL supplies the real-time environment, the server, trainer and worker split, and the replay format. Its MLPQFunction is reused as the critic class. The TD3 agent, actor, configuration and diagnostics are new code.

Warm start, then collapse

Random exploration in TrackMania mostly means hitting the first wall, so the actor is first behaviour-cloned from about 5,000 transitions of recorded human driving. The critics then train for 1,000 steps with the actor frozen before the actor is allowed to update.

Attempt 1 (19 Sep) shows why that is not enough. While frozen, the cloned actor drove properly: returns of 25–77, and episodes of up to 881 of 1,000 steps. Within about five actor updates after the warm-up ended, returns fell to exactly 0.0 with 81-step episodes, the reward function's stall timeout, and stayed there for three rounds. This is a known failure when fine-tuning a cloned policy with an off-policy critic. The critic has only seen the cloned policy's actions, so its gradients point the actor into regions where its Q estimates are meaningless.

Keeping the actor near the demonstrations

The fix follows TD3+BC: the actor loss gets a term that penalises distance from the frozen cloned actor's output, with the Q term scaled by bc_reg_alpha (2.5 here). In attempt 2, nine consecutive rounds after warm-up showed real progress and no collapse. A later commit added a linear decay of the anchor's weight, so the regulariser can loosen as the critic improves.

Plateau, not progress

Attempt 3 continued that checkpoint for about 35 minutes (90 rounds), logging metrics to CSV for the first time. Return turned out bimodal: a floor around 8–10 with 95–120-step episodes, and peaks of 40–90 every 5–10 rounds through the whole run. The BC term rose from about 0.010 to about 0.027 and levelled off, so the actor had drifted a bounded distance from the demonstrations. Critic loss rose slightly, and actor loss stayed flat.

The manifest's own conclusion is "no collapse, plateaued at a noisy but non-degenerate level". The run does not show that the car learned to drive, and that is how it is reported here.

The wall

The narrow 95–120-step band suggested a fixed obstacle, not random failure. A deterministic evaluation (no exploration noise, 6 episodes) confirmed it. Every episode accelerated cleanly to about 45 km/h by step 80, then stopped. LIDAR minimum read 0.0 on every step from 81 to 100, while the mean and maximum LIDAR distances shrank steadily: the car was wedged against the same wall in all six runs.

The mitigation is reward shaping. After 5 consecutive "stuck" steps (LIDAR minimum ≤ 0.5 and speed ≤ 2), the reward becomes −0.1, half of TMRL's 10-step failure countdown, so the signal arrives before the episode is cut. Replaying the known-bad actor against the live game showed the penalty firing on every step from the fifth stuck step until termination. That verifies the mechanism. Whether training with it teaches the policy to reverse out has not been measured.

What is tested offline

TrackMania cannot run in CI, so the pytest suite (13 test functions in 9 files) tests the parts that do not need the game: TD3 updates on synthetic batches, model shapes, checkpoint save and resume, the demonstration dataset and replay formats, curriculum promotion logic and the stuck detector. A Phase 5 smoke test against the live game confirmed real gradient updates (finite critic losses, Q around 0.47) before any longer run.

What is still open

  • A training run with the stuck penalty, comparing stuck-episode rate and return against attempt 3.
  • Curriculum learning across six custom tracks is implemented and unit-tested but has not been run, so there is no evidence about generalisation.
  • Every result above is a single run. Seeds and repeated runs are needed before comparing variants.
