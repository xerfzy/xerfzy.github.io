Xerfs, Kelvy and Uxzty privacy policy

Last updated: 23 November 2026.

This policy covers Xerfs Launcher, Kelvy Browser and Uxzty Keyboard, developed by xerfzy. Contact srecibi9@gmail.com about privacy or these apps. The developer website is xerfzy.github.io.

The public Android application IDs are com.xerfzy.xerfs (Xerfs Launcher), com.xerfzy.kelvy (Kelvy Browser) and com.xerfzy.uxzty (Uxzty Keyboard). Earlier local development versions used the Reaper names and com.example.reaper IDs. A new application ID installs as a separate app: it does not automatically copy old data, permissions, permanent rules, HOME selection or keyboard defaults.

What stays on your device

The apps provide local features without a developer online account or advertising SDK. The developer does not operate an analytics or remote crash-reporting service for them. Google Play billing and its technical diagnostics are described separately below. The apps do not send your clipboard, notes, contacts or typing to a developer-operated collection service. Network features you choose still communicate with websites, search providers, resolvers or nearby devices as explained below.

Xerfs Launcher

  • App lists, optional usage access and your choices support launching, screen-time statistics and VOID app limits. Usage information includes app identities and activity times.
  • Accessibility access is optional and is needed for advanced VOID capabilities: app-limit and Morning Mode enforcement, and address-based website filtering in supported browsers. When enabled, the service observes app and window identities and supported browser address bars, including while the launcher is in the background. Usage history supports app-time accounting, and display-over-apps access draws restriction screens. The service processes this information locally to apply the rules you choose; it does not read page forms, passwords or messages, submit forms or send service data to a developer server. A separate in-app disclosure and agreement action precede the app's link to Android Accessibility settings. Agreeing does not grant Android access automatically.
  • Notification access displays active notifications locally and supports actions you choose, such as opening, replying or dismissing. The active notification cache is held in memory and clears when the listener disconnects; it is not a remote notification archive.
  • Contacts access supports local KEX contact lookup. Calling permission is used only if you enable direct calling; otherwise calls open the dialer. Android handles the call and the other applications involved.
  • Notes, tasks, habits, reading material, command history, settings and protection rules are stored locally. Export, sharing and opening another app happen when you choose those actions.
  • The Google Play edition accesses documents you select through Android's file picker. KEX can retain read access to those documents; FORGET FILES removes its saved selection and read grants without deleting the documents. It does not request broad shared-storage access or install APKs. The full edition can use separately granted broad file access for KEX file search and folder commands, and can ask Android to install an APK you explicitly open.

Optional Xerfs Premium purchases

Optional Xerfs Premium purchases use Google Play Billing. Google Play processes the transaction under Google's terms and privacy practices; Xerfs does not receive your card or bank credentials. The Launcher receives purchase status and signed purchase information, including a purchase token, product identity and purchase time, to verify access, acknowledge completed purchases and restore purchases.

Verified receipt information and its integrity metadata are kept in private local storage excluded from the app's automatic backup and ordinary exports. Stored records remain until replaced or the app's local data is removed. Removing local app data removes this local record; a legitimate completed purchase can be restored through its purchasing Google Play account. Google's transaction records and retention are controlled separately by Google. Purchase tokens and receipts are not sent through the suite provider to Kelvy or Uxzty. The developer operates no purchase-validation server in this release.

Google Play and its Billing library process purchase-related requests and technical diagnostics to operate and troubleshoot billing. These can include app and library versions, device and operating-system information, and billing response or error information. Google's processing follows the Google Privacy Policy. These SDK diagnostics are separate from the local receipt record; removing app data does not delete records held by Google.

When checking whether a new purchase offer is available, Xerfs can request the billing country reported by Google Play. The Launcher uses that value in memory and does not store it or perform its own GPS, SIM-country or IP-location lookup for purchase eligibility. Kelvy and Uxzty do not include Google Play Billing or require a purchase to use their standalone features.

Offline wheel demonstration

After new launcher setup, an optional short demonstration shows how to use the radial wheel. The video is bundled with the app and plays offline without audio. It does not record your screen, taps or microphone, contact a video host, or send viewing analytics. You can skip it, pause it or replay it from Settings. Its example apps and visible touch indicators were recorded on a development phone; playing the video does not change your wheel assignments.

Optional permissions and Family DNS restriction screens

You can complete launcher onboarding without accessibility, usage access or display-over-apps permission. The launcher and local tools remain available; features that need declined access cannot provide system-wide enforcement. Enable accessibility only if you want the advanced VOID capabilities, through Android Settings > Accessibility. You can disable it there at any time. Notification access, contacts, direct calling, document access and enabling Uxzty Keyboard are also optional and tied to the actions described in this policy.

Safe Internet is a choice made during setup. Android's Private DNS sends device-wide domain lookups to the family resolver you select, under that resolver's own policy. The apps guide you to Android's settings; they do not silently change Private DNS. A resolver may be unreachable on some networks, and no family filter can recognize every unwanted page.

If you confirm Safe Internet, it becomes a permanent in-app commitment. When Private DNS is switched off or changed away from a family resolver, Xerfs can show a Family DNS restriction screen on HOME and, with accessibility and overlay access enabled, cover other apps until a family resolver is selected again. This is not Android's security lock screen: Xerfs does not set a PIN or replace your device screen lock. Android Settings, accessibility-disable and uninstall controls, calls and emergency calls, alarms, payment apps and Wi-Fi sign-in remain accessible. You can choose to leave adult filtering off before confirming setup.

Existing VOID rules, Safe Internet and some other confirmed choices cannot be edited or removed through normal in-app controls. This does not remove Android's controls over permissions, app storage or uninstalling. Revoking the required access stops the related enforcement; clearing app data or uninstalling removes the app-private commitments. Family DNS is an independent Android setting and can remain active after the app is removed.

Clipboard and Uxzty Keyboard

Xerfs Launcher stores the shared copied-text history in its private local storage. Uxzty Keyboard can add copied text only while its input view is visible in an active, non-password, non-incognito editor. The keyboard ignores clips explicitly marked sensitive by Android. Opening or pasting from the launcher's own clipboard panel can capture the current non-sensitive text clip.

Matching-signer suite apps can read the shared palette and copied-text history through the launcher's protected provider. This is sharing between the local suite apps, not an upload service. Unpinned clips are excluded from the active history after seven days; pruning happens when the history is read or changed, rather than by an exact deletion timer. CLEAR keeps pinned clips. Remove individual clips, including pinned clips, to delete them. The normal cap is 40 entries; pins can exceed that cap. Clips longer than 20,000 characters are not added.

Uxzty Keyboard has no Internet permission. It commits text to the application whose input field you are using. That application, websites you submit to and Android have their own data practices. Password fields hide clipboard history and key previews. Incognito protection depends on the destination editor marking the session appropriately; the keyboard cannot recognize every sensitive field on its own.

Emoji recents are local, keep up to 32 choices, and are not updated in password or correctly marked incognito sessions. These apps do not provide a cloud typing service or a developer online account.

Browser, searches and other network features

Visited websites receive normal web requests, including your network address, requested URLs and information you submit. Search engines receive the searches you choose to make. Xerfs' research and reader features can contact Wikipedia, arXiv, DuckDuckGo, OpenAlex and the publishers or repositories you open. Browser search settings can use DuckDuckGo, Google, Brave Search, Startpage or an instance you configure. Each service controls its own retention and processing.

Ordinary browser tabs can retain local cookies, site data, bookmarks, downloads and up to 300 history visits when history recording is on. Private tabs use a separate temporary WebView profile where supported; its storage is cleared when the last private tab closes. Private mode does not hide traffic from websites, DNS providers or your network. A downloaded or explicitly saved file can remain after a private tab closes.

Website camera, microphone and location features use site prompts and Android permissions. Forgetting a site's permission decision in the browser and revoking the browser's Android permission are separate controls. File uploads use Android's picker. The browser can share a reading list, palette and website-rule decisions with a matching-signer Xerfs Launcher on the same device.

Filter updates contact easylist.to when you request an update. Android WebView Safe Browsing may send hashed URL prefixes to Google and, for a suspected match, full URLs for safety checks. Availability and behavior depend on the installed WebView and Android version. Optional privacy frontends receive requests you send through them. Family Private DNS is an Android setting using your chosen third-party resolver; that resolver processes DNS requests under its own policy. Local blocking does not guarantee that every unwanted request or page is blocked.

Nearby file and text sharing

Xerfs Share advertises a device alias and connection metadata on your local network while sharing is active. Chosen peers receive selected text or files after the relevant send/receive consent. Received files are saved to shared downloads; a local receive-history list keeps up to 200 records, which can include filenames, sender alias and received text. Remove downloaded files separately using your file manager. The app's receive-history records are part of its local app data.

Xerfs' receiver uses HTTPS. Compatibility with a peer using HTTP can still involve unencrypted traffic, so this policy does not promise encryption for every network operation. The selected peer controls copies after transfer. Closing Share stops its local discovery and receiving activity.

Retention, deletion and Android controls

Local records without a stated rolling limit remain until you delete or replace them, or remove the app's local data. Browser Settings offers separate clearing of history, cookies, cache and site data. CLEAR EVERYTHING clears those categories and site permission decisions; tabs and bookmarks remain and can be closed or removed separately. Clearing download history does not erase the downloaded files.

The clipboard's CLEAR action preserves pins, and some user-chosen permanent VOID commitments are intentionally not removable through ordinary rule-editing controls. Revoking access stops future access but does not automatically erase stored local records. Android's app-storage controls or uninstalling remove app-private data; files exported or saved to shared storage and copies held by another app, person or device remain separately controlled. Clearing app data also removes local limits and settings.

Automatic Android cloud-backup and device-transfer exclusions are declared for these apps. Manufacturer transfer tools can behave differently. Explicit exports and file sharing are separate from automatic backup. The apps do not expose a separate control to clear only keyboard emoji recents; those remain until replaced by the recent-list limit or the keyboard's local app data is removed.

Your choices and contact

You can decline optional permissions, revoke Android special access, switch keyboards, stop sharing and use the available local deletion controls. Features that depend on declined access may be unavailable. If you contact the developer by email, the message and your address are handled by the email services involved; do not include passwords or unnecessary private device data.

For questions or a privacy request, email srecibi9@gmail.com. Updates to this policy will appear on this page with a revised date. The developer does not have remote access to erase data held only on your device or copies controlled by third parties.
