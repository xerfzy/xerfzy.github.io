Reaper privacy policy

Last updated: 13 November 2026.

This policy covers Reaper Launcher, Reaper Browser and Reaper Keyboard, developed by xerfzy. Contact srecibi9@gmail.com about privacy or these apps. The developer website is xerfzy.github.io.

What stays on your device

The apps provide local features without a Reaper online account. These versions contain no advertising, analytics or remote crash-reporting SDK. They do not send your clipboard, notes, contacts or typing to a developer-operated collection service. Network features you choose still communicate with websites, search providers, resolvers or nearby devices as explained below.

Reaper Launcher

  • App lists, optional usage access and your choices support launching, screen-time statistics and VOID app limits. Usage information includes app identities and activity times.
  • If enabled, the accessibility service uses app/window information and supported browser address bars to apply your app and website rules. Overlay access displays limit screens. These permissions are optional Android special access. The app does not use this access to submit website forms or upload passwords. You can disable the service in Android Accessibility settings.
  • Notification access displays active notifications locally and supports actions you choose, such as opening, replying or dismissing. The active notification cache is held in memory and clears when the listener disconnects; it is not a remote notification archive.
  • Contacts access supports local KEX contact lookup. Calling permission is used only if you enable direct calling; otherwise calls open the dialer. Android handles the call and the other applications involved.
  • Notes, tasks, habits, reading material, command history, settings and protection rules are stored locally. Export, sharing and opening another app happen when you choose those actions.
  • The Google Play edition accesses documents you select through Android's file picker. KEX can retain read access to those documents; FORGET FILES removes its saved selection and read grants without deleting the documents. It does not request broad shared-storage access or install APKs. The full edition can use separately granted broad file access for KEX file search and folder commands, and can ask Android to install an APK you explicitly open.

Clipboard and Reaper Keyboard

Reaper Launcher stores the shared copied-text history in its private local storage. Reaper Keyboard can add copied text only while its input view is visible in an active, non-password, non-incognito editor. The keyboard ignores clips explicitly marked sensitive by Android. Opening or pasting from the launcher's own clipboard panel can capture the current non-sensitive text clip.

Matching-signer Reaper apps can read the shared palette and copied-text history through the launcher's protected provider. This is sharing between the local suite apps, not an upload service. Unpinned clips are excluded from the active history after seven days; pruning happens when the history is read or changed, rather than by an exact deletion timer. CLEAR keeps pinned clips. Remove individual clips, including pinned clips, to delete them. The normal cap is 40 entries; pins can exceed that cap. Clips longer than 20,000 characters are not added.

Reaper Keyboard has no Internet permission. It commits text to the application whose input field you are using. That application, websites you submit to and Android have their own data practices. Password fields hide clipboard history and key previews. Incognito protection depends on the destination editor marking the session appropriately; the keyboard cannot recognize every sensitive field on its own.

Emoji recents are local, keep up to 32 choices, and are not updated in password or correctly marked incognito sessions. These apps do not provide a cloud typing service or a Reaper online account.

Browser, searches and other network features

Visited websites receive normal web requests, including your network address, requested URLs and information you submit. Search engines receive the searches you choose to make. Reaper's research and reader features can contact Wikipedia, arXiv, DuckDuckGo, OpenAlex and the publishers or repositories you open. Browser search settings can use DuckDuckGo, Google, Brave Search, Startpage or an instance you configure. Each service controls its own retention and processing.

Ordinary browser tabs can retain local cookies, site data, bookmarks, downloads and up to 300 history visits when history recording is on. Private tabs use a separate temporary WebView profile where supported; its storage is cleared when the last private tab closes. Private mode does not hide traffic from websites, DNS providers or your network. A downloaded or explicitly saved file can remain after a private tab closes.

Website camera, microphone and location features use site prompts and Android permissions. Forgetting a site's permission decision in the browser and revoking the browser's Android permission are separate controls. File uploads use Android's picker. The browser can share a reading list, palette and website-rule decisions with a matching-signer Reaper Launcher on the same device.

Filter updates contact easylist.to when you request an update. Android WebView Safe Browsing may exchange URL-related safety information with its service provider. Optional privacy frontends receive requests you send through them. Family Private DNS is an Android setting using your chosen third-party resolver; that resolver processes DNS requests under its own policy. Local blocking does not guarantee that every unwanted request or page is blocked.

Nearby file and text sharing

Reaper Share advertises a device alias and connection metadata on your local network while sharing is active. Chosen peers receive selected text or files after the relevant send/receive consent. Received files are saved to shared downloads; a local receive-history list keeps up to 200 records, which can include filenames, sender alias and received text. Remove downloaded files separately using your file manager. The app's receive-history records are part of its local app data.

Reaper's receiver uses HTTPS. Compatibility with a peer using HTTP can still involve unencrypted traffic, so this policy does not promise encryption for every network operation. The selected peer controls copies after transfer. Closing Share stops its local discovery and receiving activity.

Retention, deletion and Android controls

Local records without a stated rolling limit remain until you delete or replace them, or remove the app's local data. Browser Settings offers separate clearing of history, cookies, cache and site data. CLEAR EVERYTHING clears those categories and site permission decisions; tabs and bookmarks remain and can be closed or removed separately. Clearing download history does not erase the downloaded files.

The clipboard's CLEAR action preserves pins, and some user-chosen permanent VOID commitments are intentionally not removable through ordinary rule-editing controls. Revoking access stops future access but does not automatically erase stored local records. Android's app-storage controls or uninstalling remove app-private data; files exported or saved to shared storage and copies held by another app, person or device remain separately controlled. Clearing app data also removes local limits and settings.

Automatic Android cloud-backup and device-transfer exclusions are declared for these apps. Manufacturer transfer tools can behave differently. Explicit exports and file sharing are separate from automatic backup. The apps do not expose a separate control to clear only keyboard emoji recents; those remain until replaced by the recent-list limit or the keyboard's local app data is removed.

Your choices and contact

You can decline optional permissions, revoke Android special access, switch keyboards, stop sharing and use the available local deletion controls. Features that depend on declined access may be unavailable. If you contact the developer by email, the message and your address are handled by the email services involved; do not include passwords or unnecessary private device data.

For questions or a privacy request, email srecibi9@gmail.com. Updates to this policy will appear on this page with a revised date. The developer does not have remote access to erase data held only on your device or copies controlled by third parties.
